01

The idea

ஆழி என்றால் ஆழ்கடல்.

ஆழி is an old Tamil word for the sea. Not the beach. The deep part, where the light runs out and the good stuff lives.

That is the promise. Most tech content is surface: reels, roadmaps, "learn X in 10 minutes". We take a student from a town with one bus stand and walk them, in Tamil, all the way down to a real engineering job. The ocean gives us one metaphor that explains the whole product, so nobody needs a pricing page to understand what is free and what is not.

Why the sea, and why it is ours

Roots

Thoothukudi knows divers.

Thoothukudi is still called the Pearl City. For centuries, divers off this coast went down into the Gulf of Mannar on a single breath and came up with pearls. Skill, nerve, training, and a crew on the boat. That is exactly what a cohort is.

Coast

Every Tamil kid knows the sea.

Marina on a school trip. Tiruchendur with the family. Rameswaram, Kanyakumari, Nagapattinam. Even the inland kids in Karur and Salem have stood at the water once. The metaphor needs no explaining in our ooru.

Gen Z

Depth beats surface.

This generation is ambitious and tired of hype. "Don't scroll the surface, dive" says it without a guru voice. And diving is honest: it takes breath, practice and time. We never pretend the deep part is easy. We just make sure nobody dives alone.

Naming rules

  • Tier names lead in Tamil script in display type: கரை, அலை, முத்துக்குளி. UI labels use the transliteration: Karai, Alai, Muthukuli.
  • Never translate the tiers into English product names. No "Pearl Program", no "Wave Plan".
  • Cohorts are numbered pearls: Muthu 01, Muthu 02, Muthu 03. Always two digits.
  • Brand name in Latin is Aazhi in sentences and aazhi. as the lockup. Never "Azhi", "Aazi", "AAZHI".

02

Three visual worlds

மூணு உலகம். ஒரே குரல்.

Every screen, post and poster lives in exactly one world. Pick the world by what the moment is about, not by what looks cool this week. Mixing two worlds in one viewport is allowed only at a section seam, like a sheet of paper lying on the edge of a ticket.

03

Colour

மிளகாய், பட்டு, காகிதம், பனி.

Seventeen colours, all pulled from the three references. No pure white, no pure black, no new hues. Tints are fine (a 60% paper for tape), new colours are not. Tap a chip to copy its hex.

Grounds the big fields a world sits on

  • மிளகாய்Chilli --chilli Paper Desk field. Ink text only on it.
  • செகண்ட் ஷோOxblood --oxblood Ticket Night poster. Paper, fog, lavender, salmon text.
  • நோட்டுPaper --paper Sheets, tickets, reading surfaces. Our "white".
  • பனிFog --fog Code Portrait ground. Flat, never textured beyond grain.
  • ஆழம்Sea --sea The deep. Rare: footer, the pearl moment, max one block per page.

Inks what we write with

  • கரிInk --ink Body text, wordmark, primary buttons.
  • GraphiteInk 2 --ink-2 Secondary text on paper.
  • PencilInk 3 --ink-3 Meta and captions on paper only (4.84).
  • பேனாBiro --biro Hand notes, link underlines, focus on paper.
  • முத்திரைStamp --stamp Rubber stamps and error text on paper.

Accents highlighters, ribbons, depth

  • பட்டுMaroon --maroon Mid-tone of the ticket fade. Tamil headings on paper.

When Tamil script leads

  1. Emotional lines lead in Tamil. Hero, section openers, success moments, the footer. English follows underneath, smaller: ஊர் சின்னது. கனவு பெருசு. / Small town. Deep code.
  2. Tier names lead in Tamil in display, with the transliteration as a narrow-caps label: கரை / KARAI.
  3. Instructions lead in Tanglish or English. Prices, steps, errors, forms, legal. Clarity first: many of our students read UI faster in Latin script.
  4. Size Tamil at 0.85× the Anton next to it. Tamil glyphs carry vowel signs above and below, so they look bigger. Give them 1.1 leading, never 0.88.
  5. Every Tamil line must mean something. No decorative Tamil, no machine translation. A Tamil speaker reads it out loud before it ships. Mark it up with lang="ta".

05

Texture & props

மேசை மேல இருக்குறது.

Depth comes from real things on a real desk, not from shadows on rounded boxes. Every prop below is CSS or inline SVG, so it scales, themes and costs nothing to load. Two props per sheet, max. Rotate between −3° and 3°.

  • no graingrain

    Film grain

    background-image: var(--grain)

    On every chilli, fog and oxblood field, as an overlay at 15–25% multiply. Kills the flat-digital look.

  • Paper fibres

    background-image: var(--fibers)

    On every sheet and ticket. Long horizontal fibres, like cheap A4 from the xerox kadai.

  • Paperclip

    <use href="#i-clip"/>

    Holds a note to a sheet. Top edge, overlapping by a third. Ink-2 wire.

  • Binder clip

    <use href="#i-binder"/>

    Holds a whole stack. One per stack, top centre. Means "this is a set".

  • Karai · 03

    Tape & torn label

    .tape · .tape-label

    Clear tape (60% paper tint) pins corners. A torn paper label names a sheet in narrow caps.

  • Code-ku grammar podhum, accent illa.

    Highlighter with handles

    .hl.hl-handles

    Lavender or salmon under ink. The biro selection handles say "a person chose this". One per paragraph.

  • Sold out

    Rubber stamp

    .stamp

    Status only: Sold out, Live batch, Seats filling, Paid, Batch open. Anton in stamp red, distressed, rotated 6–12°. On paper, never on oxblood directly.

  • Class starts at 7 PM sharp.

    Hand ellipse

    <use href="#i-ellipse"/>

    One loose biro loop around the single word that matters. Never around a whole paragraph.

  • Halftone & bookmark

    #i-pearl · .bookmark

    Halftone dots for emblems (the pearl). A violet ribbon peeks out from under a sheet to mark "start here".

  • Resume Review · 3 per day

    Torn edge

    .torn

    A note ripped from a notebook. Tools and small asides, like the free Resume Review.

Don't.

இது வேண்டாம். ப்ளீஸ்.

  • Box-grid backgrounds. Graph paper, square grids, dot grids behind content. They read as an AI template from 2023. Use grain, fibres, stacked paper and torn edges.

  • Neat SaaS card rows. Three rounded cards, icon on top, three lines of text, soft shadow. If it could be any startup's site, it isn't ours.

  • Gradients. Purple-to-blue glows, mesh blobs, glassy fades. The one exception is Ticket Night's oxblood → cream poster fade, and it always carries grain.

  • Emoji icons. No rockets, sparkles, fire or "feature icon + 3 lines" blocks. We draw with type, props and ASCII.

Also no: pure #fff or #000 fields · drop shadows on text · rotations past 3° (stamps excepted) · Tamil set in Anton · stock photos of people pointing at laptops · "fam", "slay", "no cap".

Voice & tone

நம்ம ஊர் அண்ணன், அக்கா மாதிரி பேசு.

Who we talk to

18 to 23. Often the first engineer in the family. Living a bus ride from the nearest IT park.

Some went to Tamil-medium schools and freeze when an interviewer switches to English. Some share one phone or one laptop with a sibling. They watch Tamil tech YouTube at 1.5×, live on reels, and hear "settle aagu" from home every Sunday. They are not beginners at life. They are beginners at this, and they are tired of being talked down to.

Our voice: a sharp senior from your own ooru

The anna or akka from your street who made it to a product company and came back to pull you up. Five traits, each with its edge.

  • Senior-from-your-ooru

    நம்ம ஊர் சீனியர்

    "I took the same 6:40 bus. Here's what I'd do."

    Is specific, local, been-there. Isn't a guru with a ring light.

  • Confident

    தைரியம்

    "Nine months. Real backend. You can do this."

    Is sure of the plan. Isn't "only 1% make it" arrogance.

  • Warm

    அக்கறை

    "Saapdiya? Okay. Now one more commit."

    Is Amma-level care. Isn't syrupy or therapy-speak.

  • Cheeky

    குறும்பு

    "Works on my machine-a? Unga machine-a ship panna mudiyaadhu."

    Is one wink per screen. Isn't memes for memes' sake.

  • Honest

    நேர்மை

    "Free means free. ₹14,999 means ₹14,999. Nine months means nine months."

    Is numbers up front. Isn't fake timers or "guaranteed placement".

Tanglish spelling, so we all write it the same way

  • aa for long a: paaru, venaam. zh for ழ: Aazhi. dh for soft த: podhum.
  • English word + Tamil suffix takes a hyphen: phone-la, code-ku, arrear-oda.
  • Gender-neutral by default: nanba / nanbi together. Never only "da" or only "di". Machi at most once per screen.
