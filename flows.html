Flows: கரை → அலை → முத்து

shore → wave → pearl  /  free → hooked → hired

30 Oct 2026owner: product designstatus: rough, but argue with it

The principle

Free first. Lesson 1 opens with no login wall, and every upgrade moment is one we can defend to a student's Amma.

A 19-year-old in Kovilpatti taps a reel on a shared phone with two bars of Jio. Within two taps they should be inside lesson 1: no signup, no OTP, no form. We ask for an account only when it does something for them (saving progress at lesson 3, so it survives a phone swap). We ask for money only at honest moments: they finished a course, they sat a live class, or their resume score told them the truth. The free path is always shown next to the paid one. Every screen is designed for a 6-inch phone on patchy 4G first: light pages, text-first notes, low-data video, progress that survives a dropped connection, and accounts that make it obvious whose phone it is.

கரை first. Then ஆழம்.

  • Free first
  • No login · lesson 1
  • Honest upgrade
  • Phone + 4G first

Read F1 as a list
  1. Entry: taps a link from an Instagram reel (link sticker), a YouTube video (pinned comment) or a WhatsApp class-group forward.
  2. Lands on the course page /c/f-python inside the in-app browser, not on Home. Copy: "Lesson 1 ippove paaru. Login venaam."
    • Why: deep-link to the course; every extra tap on 4G loses people.
    • Drop-off risk: blank screen for seconds on a slow in-app browser. Fix: under 150 KB first paint, text first, poster image, no autoplay video.
  3. Decision: is the network strong? Detected automatically. Yes: 480p video. No: low-data mode (240p, or audio plus notes), with a visible data estimate ("≈ 9 MB").
    • Why: the data pack is someone's money; show the MB before it's spent.
  4. Lesson 1 player, 8 minutes: Tamil class, English notes under the video, code runs in the browser. Copy: "Phone-la type pannu. Laptop appuram."
    • Drop-off risk: typing code on a phone keyboard. Fix: snippet chips for brackets, colons and quotes; fill-the-blank exercises before full typing.
  5. First output moment: print("vanakkam") runs. Copy: "Un first program. Semma."
    • Why: a first win inside 10 minutes, or we lose them to the next reel.
  6. Decision: start lesson 2? Yes: continue. Not now: they leave, progress is kept on the device, and we offer "Link-a WhatsApp-la unakke anuppu" (send the link to yourself, no signup).
    • Drop-off risk: they return on a sibling's phone and progress is gone. This is the hook for F2: saving means progress follows you.
  7. Goal: lesson 2 starts, still without login. Hands off to F2 (soft gate at lesson 3).

Read F2 as a list
  1. Entry: lesson 2 finished without an account, either straight on from F1, via a self-shared WhatsApp link, or on the same phone the next day.
  2. Lesson 3 opens. After 60 seconds a bottom sheet asks: "2 lessons mudichitta! Save pannalaama? Phone maarinaalum continue aagum."
    • Why: gate at lesson 3, not lesson 1, because they have felt the value. Ask after the lesson starts, never before it.
  3. Decision: save now? Yes: continue to sign-in. "Ippo venaam": skip is allowed, lesson 3 continues; lesson 4 onwards needs a free account, and that is said up front.
  4. Decision: phone OTP or Google? Phone OTP is the default. Google one-tap shows the account email large with a "Vera account use pannu" option.
    • Why: on a shared phone the signed-in Google account is often a parent's.
  5. Phone number and OTP, auto-read. Copy: "30 sec-la varalanna WhatsApp-la anuppuvom."
    • Drop-off risk: SMS is late, blocked by DND, or the SIM has no balance. Fix: resend at 30 seconds, WhatsApp OTP, voice-call fallback.
  6. Welcome screen with two fields (name, year; college and town optional). Copy: "Unga per enna? Certificate-la idhu dhaan varum." Device progress merges: lessons 1 and 2 saved.
  7. Decision: WhatsApp reminders? Unticked by default. Yes: pick a slot. No: in-app streak only; we ask once more after day 7.
    • Why: opt-in only, replying STOP works, consent is logged.
  8. Slot picker matched to their day: the 6:40 morning bus, 7 PM after college, or 9:30 PM after dinner.
  9. Goal: a 7-day streak, drawn as kolam dots. One lesson or ten minutes of practice counts as a day; one free streak freeze per week. Leads into F5.
    • Why: a streak that punishes a power cut is a streak they quit.
    • Drop-off risk: the day 3–4 slump, exams, power cuts. Fix: a day-3 nudge with a five-minute lesson; the freeze applies automatically in exam weeks they mark.

Read F3 as a list
  1. Entry, three triggers: T1, they finish a free course; T2, they attend the "Try one class free" live session; T3, their Resume Review score is below 70.
    • T2 detail: the live class runs audio-first with slides for low data, and ends with real Q&A, a mentor introduction and a two-minute pitch, with no countdown timer.
    • T3 detail: a score of 62 lists the gaps (no Git link, nothing deployed) and points to the free fix first (Karai Git and Docker), with the cohort shown as the guided route.
  2. Honest upgrade card. Copy: "Karai mudinjichu! Adutha step paid: mentor, mocks, review. Free-la continue pannalaam, adhuvum OK."
    • Why: every upgrade card also shows the free next step; pushing paid on a weak score costs trust across the whole town.
  3. Cohort page styled as a ticket: Muthu 01, ₹14,999 or ₹1,999 a month, batch 16 Nov, 17 seats left (the real count, no fake countdown).
    • Drop-off risk: price shock. Fix: show full price and EMI together, with the scholarship link right under them.
  4. Free versus paid comparison, side by side. Paid adds live Tamil classes, a 1:20 mentor ratio, two scored mock interviews, capstone review and the referral board.
    • Why: also say what paid does not add (same notes, same videos). Trust beats conversion.
  5. Decision: a three-question fit check (year, hours per week, laptop). Fit: continue. Not yet: join the waitlist with a plan ("Python + Git mudichittu vaa"), pinged 10 days before the next batch.
    • Drop-off risk: no laptop. Fix: say clearly before payment that weeks 1–6 work on a phone and a laptop is needed from week 7.
  6. Seat picker: reserve a seat for ₹0, held for 48 hours. Copy: "Seat 11 unakku hold pannirukkom. Veetla pesu."
    • Why: money is a family decision; a free hold gives time to talk to parents.
    • Drop-off risk: the hold expires quietly. Fix: reminders at 24 hours and 4 hours, plus a ready-made parent-share link.
  7. Goal: seat held. Hands off to F4 checkout.
